# Research: Auroras & Solar Wind

## 1. Normal Solar Wind
- **NASA Source**: NASA Heliophysics Directorate (heliophysics.gsfc.nasa.gov)
- **Scientific Claim**: The Sun constantly releases a stream of charged particles (electrons and protons) known as the solar wind.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"The Sun is always sending out a stream of tiny invisible particles into space. We call this the solar wind."*
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 2. Earth's Magnetosphere
- **NASA Source**: NASA MMS Mission / Space Physics (mms.gsfc.nasa.gov)
- **Scientific Claim**: Earth's intrinsic magnetic field creates a protective magnetosphere that channels incoming charged solar wind particles along magnetic field lines toward the polar regions.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"Earth has an invisible magnetic shield around it. It funnels incoming space particles toward the North and South Poles."*
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 3. Atmospheric Excitation & Aurora Colors
- **NASA Source**: NASA Science Mission Directorate — Aurora Science
- **Scientific Claim**: Incoming energetic particles collide with oxygen and nitrogen atoms in Earth's upper atmosphere (ionosphere/thermosphere). The collisions excite the gas molecules, which emit photons of light upon returning to ground state:
  - Green (557.7 nm): Atomic oxygen at ~100–300 km altitude (most common)
  - Pink/Red (630.0 nm): Oxygen at higher altitudes (>300 km)
  - Purple/Violet: Molecular nitrogen ionized by high-energy collisions
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"When particles from space bump into air high above Earth, the air glows like a giant neon sign! Green comes from oxygen, purple from nitrogen."*
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 4. Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs)
- **NASA Source**: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center — CME Science
- **Scientific Claim**: CMEs are massive eruptions of solar plasma and magnetic field from the Sun's corona. Unlike solar flares (which travel at the speed of light), CMEs travel at 250 to 3,000 km/s and take 1 to 5 days to reach Earth. Strong CMEs cause geomagnetic storms.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"Sometimes the Sun has a giant storm and sends out a huge cloud of energy and material. When it reaches Earth, the auroras become brighter and reach further south!"*
- **Verification Status**: Verified
