# Research: Technology & Human Impacts

## 1. Global Positioning System (GPS / GNSS)
- **NASA Source**: NASA Space Weather / SWPC & Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)
- **Scientific Claim**: Geomagnetic storms cause rapid density fluctuations in Earth's ionosphere (ionospheric scintillation and total electron content changes). This causes GPS radio signals to refract and delay, reducing positioning accuracy from meters to tens of meters.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"Strong space weather can make signals between satellites and ground receivers slightly delayed, making GPS locations less precise."*
- **Narrative Application**: Shown through Lina's handheld navigation app showing a temporary location uncertainty ring.
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 2. Radio Communications
- **NASA Source**: NASA Space Weather Overview — HF Radio Disruption
- **Scientific Claim**: Increased ionization in the lower ionosphere (D-region) caused by solar X-rays or energetic particles absorbs High Frequency (HF) radio waves rather than reflecting them back to Earth.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"High-frequency radio signals bounce off the upper atmosphere. When space weather is active, those signals can get absorbed, causing temporary static."*
- **Narrative Application**: Lina hears static on her campsite emergency radio during the peak event.
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 3. Satellites in Orbit
- **NASA Source**: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center — Space Weather Effects on Spacecraft
- **Scientific Claim**: Enhanced upper atmosphere heating during geomagnetic storms causes atmospheric expansion (increased atmospheric drag on Low Earth Orbit satellites), and increased energetic particle fluxes can cause surface charging or single-event upsets (SEUs) in satellite electronics.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"Satellites orbiting high above Earth must be designed to withstand streams of energetic particles during space weather storms."*
- **Narrative Application**: Visible in the space transition as satellites orbiting around Earth's magnetic cocoon.
- **Verification Status**: Verified

## 4. Power Systems & Electrical Grids
- **NASA Source**: NASA / USGS Geomagnetic Induced Current (GIC) Studies
- **Scientific Claim**: Rapidly fluctuating magnetic fields during severe geomagnetic storms induce electric fields in the Earth's crust, driving Geomagnetic Induced Currents (GICs) into long, grounded high-voltage power lines.
- **Child-Friendly Explanation**: *"Changing magnetic fields can create electric currents in long power lines on Earth, requiring power grid operators to carefully monitor system balance."*
- **Narrative Application**: Shown in the world returning to Lina as distant town lights dimming slightly or being monitored by power operators.
- **Verification Status**: Verified
