## ✨ What is this?

*Stellar Stories* is an interactive children's storybook that teaches kids **what space weather is** and **how it touches real life here on Earth** — told through the eyes of a young photographer who lives where auroras dance.

Our story follows **Lina, 14**, an aspiring photographer in Tromsø, Norway. One freezing night she captures a spectacular aurora — and a tiny spark of light in her photograph starts asking questions. So we zoom inside the light and travel all the way to the Sun, then all the way back, to discover how a distant star makes our own skies glow.

It's built for curious kids (ages 8–12), with real pictures, gentle "tap to keep going" interactions, and science explained in the way a story explains things: one small idea at a time.

---

## 🌍 The heart of it: space weather touches everyone

Space weather isn't just about pretty lights. The Sun is always sending us a stream of invisible particles, and storms it launches can ripple all the way to Earth. Different people feel that in different ways:

- 👩‍🌾 **Farmers** — a storm can scramble the GPS a tractor uses to plant a straight row.
- ✈️ **Pilots** — radio and navigation signals can get a little fuzzy high in the sky.
- 👨‍🚀 **Astronauts** — our travelers in space feel space weather the most directly.
- 🔌 **Power grid operators** — big storms need careful watching, so the lights stay on.
- 📷 **Lina the photographer** — and our story's own way in: a girl who only wanted a good photo of the night.

Lina is **one example among many**. She's our guide — a friendly face to show a child the way in. But the lesson underneath is for everyone: the Sun and the Earth are connected, and what happens far away can reach our everyday lives.

## 🔭 A story that starts with a photograph

The experience is one continuous, cinematic journey:

1. **A photograph.** Lina captures the aurora over Tromsø.
2. **A spark.** A tiny light in her picture says, *"See that tiny spark? That's me."*
3. **Into the sky.** We follow that spark as it makes the atmosphere glow like a giant neon sign.
4. **Into the dark.** Empty space, on a long journey back toward the Sun.
5. **The Sun.** Where the spark was born — a storm, a cloud of particles, sent our way.
6. **Back to Earth.** The spark found Earth, and the aurora danced brighter that night.
7. **Why it matters.** What space weather means for people up high and down here on the ground.
8. **The ending.** Same night, same photograph. *Every aurora tells a story.*

Along the way, small credits point to the real NASA science behind every idea.

## 📚 Learn more about the real science

Everything in *Stellar Stories* is grounded in NASA's own words. Here's where the magic is explained properly (and where our credits point):

- [What is Space Weather?](https://science.nasa.gov/heliophysics/focus-areas/space-weather/) — NASA's friendly introduction to the space environment between the Sun and Earth.
- [Solar Storms and Flares](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/solar-storms-and-flares/) — how the Sun stirs things up and sends energy our way.
- [NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/) — the people who watch the Sun and forecast what's coming.
- [Auroras from Space](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/10809/) — NASA visualization: the northern lights seen from above.
- [Aurora Sightings Around the World](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11179/) — auroras captured from Earth and orbit.
- [Charged Particles and the Aurora](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/10959/) — the science of light in the sky.

These are the same NASA sources our story cites, chosen so children (and the grown-ups reading with them) can see the real thing.
